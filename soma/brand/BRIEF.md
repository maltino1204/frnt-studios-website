# SOMA — Complete Brand Identity & Creative Direction (client brief, verbatim)

> Saved by the Lead on 2026-11-01. The binding source for the brand workstream. Do not edit; add decisions to the brand documents instead.

THE MISSION

Create a complete, distinctive and premium brand identity for SOMA, a mobile-first marketplace connecting customers with independent beauty and wellness professionals who deliver treatments at home, in hotels and at offices.

This is a full branding assignment, not merely a logo exercise.

Create an integrated visual and verbal identity that can be applied consistently to a consumer application, provider platform, website, social media, digital advertising and future physical touchpoints.

The result must feel like an established international premium lifestyle brand.

Working title: SOMA

Primary tagline: Your body. Your place.

Brand descriptor: Beauty & Wellness, wherever you are.

The name SOMA derives from the ancient Greek word for body. Use this as a conceptual foundation without making the identity feel medical, spiritual, esoteric or overly literal.

1. BRAND STRATEGY

Brand essence

Personal care, on your terms.

SOMA transforms professional beauty and wellness treatments into an effortless, location-independent experience.

The brand exists to bring professional care into people's everyday lives without compromising on quality, aesthetics or convenience.

Positioning

A premium, accessible beauty and wellness concierge powered by technology.

SOMA must sit between three worlds:

* Luxury hospitality: service quality, comfort, discretion and attention to detail.
* Contemporary beauty and wellness: self-care, confidence, physical wellbeing and personal rituals.
* Modern consumer technology: simplicity, accessibility, efficiency and seamless digital interactions.

Target audiences

Primary: Urban, design-conscious adults aged approximately 25–55 who value convenience, quality and time.

Secondary: Busy professionals, frequent travelers, couples, hotel guests and customers booking recurring personal care treatments.

Supply-side: Independent beauty and wellness professionals who want more bookings and a professional digital business infrastructure.

Brand personality

Sophisticated but approachable.

Confident but never arrogant.

Sensual but never provocative.

Premium but never pretentious.

Modern but not trend-dependent.

Calm but never boring.

Brand values

Quality, convenience, trust, independence, personal wellbeing and considered simplicity.

2. CREATIVE DIRECTION

The overall aesthetic is contemporary European luxury.

Imagine a beautifully art-directed independent magazine, a refined boutique hotel and a premium beauty brand translated into a world-class digital interface.

References may include contemporary luxury hospitality, high-end editorial design, premium cosmetics and modern architectural photography.

Do not imitate any existing brand.

Avoid conventional spa clichés, lotus flowers, generic leaves, water droplets, mystical symbolism, excessive gradients, overly feminine styling and obvious wellness iconography.

The identity must remain suitable for massage, nails, skincare, hair styling and future services.

It must not look like a nail salon.

It must not look like a medical clinic.

It must not look like a conventional SaaS product.

3. LOGO DESIGN

Develop a custom SOMA logo system.

Primary wordmark

Create a refined, typography-led SOMA wordmark.

Explore custom letterforms, exceptional kerning, balanced proportions and subtle optical corrections.

The logo must feel original and recognizable without relying on decorative effects.

Explore three distinct creative directions:

Direction A — Architectural

A sophisticated, precise, modern wordmark with strong structural proportions and elegant negative space.

Direction B — Editorial

A luxury editorial wordmark combining distinctive typographic character with contemporary restraint.

Direction C — Organic Modernism

A softer, sculptural interpretation with subtle curves referencing the human body, comfort and physical wellbeing.

Evaluate all three directions against originality, elegance, scalability and memorability.

Select the strongest direction and refine it.

Secondary mark

Create a distinctive compact brand symbol suitable for an app icon, favicon, avatar and small-scale applications.

Explore an abstract S, an abstracted letterform or a unique negative-space construction.

The symbol must be visually connected to the wordmark and should not depend on generic wellness imagery.

Logo system deliverables

* Primary horizontal wordmark
* Secondary logo variation
* Standalone symbol
* App icon
* Favicon
* Light and dark versions
* Monochrome versions
* Minimum size rules
* Clear space rules
* Incorrect usage examples

Create clean SVG master assets and appropriate raster exports.

All logo artwork must remain editable and scalable.

4. COLOR IDENTITY

Develop the brand around the following proposed starting palette.

Primary background — Bone: #F3EFE8

Secondary background — Stone: #D8CFC3

Supporting neutral — Taupe: #A69A8D

Signature accent — Oxblood: #542C35

Primary dark — Espresso: #25211F

The palette should feel warm, tactile, luxurious and confident.

Bone and Espresso should provide the main interface foundation.

Oxblood should be the signature brand accent for selective high-impact moments, important calls to action and brand recognition.

Use neutral tonal variations for backgrounds, separators, cards and surfaces.

Create a complete accessible color token system including text, borders, interactive states, feedback and focus indicators.

Do not rely on color alone to communicate status.

Validate contrast ratios and adjust functional colors where necessary.

Avoid using the accent color everywhere.

Restraint is essential.

5. TYPOGRAPHY

Typography is one of the most important parts of the identity.

Develop a two-font typographic system.

Display typography

Use an elegant editorial display typeface with distinctive character and premium proportions.

Explore legally usable, web-compatible serif or refined high-contrast alternatives.

Interface typography

Use a precise, modern sans-serif with excellent readability at small sizes.

Potential directions include Geist, Inter or similarly clean functional typefaces.

Do not automatically adopt these fonts without comparison.

Select the final pairing based on visual harmony, readability, performance, licensing and multilingual support.

Define:

* Display headings
* H1–H6
* Introductory copy
* Body text
* Small text
* Eyebrows
* Buttons
* Labels
* Form controls
* Numeric displays

Specify font weights, line heights, optical sizing, tracking and responsive typography.

Use carefully calibrated fluid typography rather than arbitrary font sizes.

6. PHOTOGRAPHY & ART DIRECTION

Photography should feel cinematic, intimate and editorial.

Focus on:

* Natural human skin and authentic details
* Hands, movement, touch and texture
* Beautiful residential spaces
* Architectural interiors
* Natural daylight and controlled shadows
* Professional treatments in believable environments
* Discreet moments of personal care

The visual narrative should communicate convenience and wellbeing without looking staged.

Create consistency through lighting, composition, color grading and image selection.

Avoid:

* Generic smiling stock photography
* Exaggerated spa imagery
* Artificial-looking portraits
* Heavy skin retouching
* Sterile treatment environments
* Inconsistent visual styles

Use appropriately licensed imagery.

Never copy competitor assets.

If final imagery is unavailable, use clearly identified development assets and document replacement requirements.

7. GRAPHIC LANGUAGE

Develop a restrained and recognizable graphic system.

Explore subtle sculptural geometry, sophisticated crops, editorial grids and abstract shapes inspired by human form and movement.

Graphic elements should support storytelling, never become visual decoration without purpose.

Consider one proprietary SOMA visual motif that can translate across digital interfaces, motion graphics and social media.

Develop a coherent iconography system with consistent line weight, proportions and optical alignment.

8. MOTION IDENTITY

Motion should reinforce the feeling of effortless care.

Characteristics:

* Fluid
* Controlled
* Subtle
* Responsive
* Intuitive
* Premium

Create motion principles for page transitions, overlays, navigation, loading states, interactive elements and brand animations.

Avoid excessive bounce, distracting parallax, unnecessary 3D elements and animation that delays interaction.

Honor reduced-motion preferences.

9. UI DESIGN SYSTEM

Create a complete token-based design system suitable for the future SOMA marketplace.

Include:

* Colors and semantic tokens
* Typography
* Spacing
* Grid and breakpoints
* Border radii
* Shadows
* Iconography
* Buttons
* Input fields
* Search controls
* Navigation
* Service cards
* Professional profile cards
* Date and time selectors
* Booking summaries
* Modals and drawers
* Notifications
* Form feedback
* Loading and empty states
* Responsive and accessible component patterns

Maintain a premium visual identity without compromising everyday usability.

Functional UI must remain clear and frictionless.

10. VERBAL IDENTITY

The brand should speak naturally and confidently.

German is the primary product language.

English may be used sparingly for distinctive campaign lines and brand expressions.

The writing style should be concise, elegant, contemporary and human.

Avoid corporate jargon, forced luxury vocabulary, empty advertising statements and exaggerated promises.

Use direct language.

Proposed tagline:

Your body. Your place.

Additional messaging directions:

* Zeit für dich. Wo du willst.
* Beauty & Wellness. Bei dir.
* Dein Moment. Dein Ort.
* Professionelle Treatments. Ganz entspannt bei dir.

These are starting points, not mandatory final slogans.

Develop a compact brand voice guide including sample headlines, service descriptions, CTAs, error states and customer communications.

11. BRAND APPLICATIONS

Demonstrate the identity across realistic use cases:

1. Landing page
2. Mobile customer application
3. Professional dashboard
4. iOS and Android app icons
5. Social media profile and editorial posts
6. Digital advertising
7. Booking confirmation
8. Transactional email
9. Gift card
10. Professional onboarding materials

Every application must feel recognizably SOMA.

12. MULTI-AGENT WORKFLOW

Create four specialized agents or isolated workstreams where supported:

Brand Strategist: Positioning, audience, messaging and competitive differentiation.

Identity Designer: Logo exploration, typography, colors and graphic language.

Digital Art Director: Photography, motion, brand applications and UI translation.

Independent Brand Auditor: Critically evaluates originality, consistency, usability and production readiness.

Coordinate their work through a single creative direction.

Use independent reviews, not superficial agreement between agents.

13. QUALITY CONTROL

Evaluate the identity across:

* Originality
* Visual sophistication
* Brand memorability
* Typography
* Logo scalability
* Color coherence
* Digital usability
* Accessibility
* Cross-platform consistency
* Long-term relevance

Rate each dimension from 0–10 using documented reasoning.

The target is at least 8/10 in every category.

Revise weak areas and repeat the review.

Do not claim perfect scores without evidence.

14. FINAL DELIVERABLES

Produce:

* Brand strategy document
* Complete brand guidelines
* Three logo concepts and one refined final direction
* SVG and raster logo assets
* App icon and favicon assets
* Color and typography specifications
* Photography direction
* Graphic and motion principles
* Brand voice guide
* Responsive digital design system
* Design token files usable in the application
* Example brand applications
* Independent quality audit

Create structured project files such as BRAND_GUIDELINES.md, DESIGN_TOKENS, LOGO_USAGE.md and the appropriate asset directories.

Use the original SVG assets as authoritative logo masters.

Keep the identity internally consistent.

FINAL INSTRUCTION

This is a complete premium branding assignment.

Start by examining the existing project and its assets.

Preserve functional infrastructure and relevant previous work.

Develop the brand system before designing application or website components.

Do not stop after producing moodboards or generic branding recommendations.

Create the actual usable brand assets and design foundations.

Do not publish externally or purchase fonts, assets or services without approval.

SOMA must emerge as an original, cohesive and exceptionally well-considered consumer brand.
